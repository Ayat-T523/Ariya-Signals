Short answer. GERD-204 met its primary endpoint with a clinically meaningful margin over placebo and was broadly consistent with the Phase II readout. Two secondary endpoints warrant careful framing for the EMA briefing.

1Primary endpoint

Proportion of subjects with complete symptom resolution at Week 8 was 62.4% on GERD-204 vs 34.1% on placebo (Δ 28.3 pp, 95% CI 22.1–34.4; p<0.001) 12. The effect was consistent across Baseline severity strata, with a slightly larger absolute benefit in the severe subgroup 3.

2Key secondary endpoints

  • GSRS total score change from baseline — -3.8 on GERD-204 vs -1.4 on placebo (p<0.001) 2. Exceeded the prespecified MCID of 1.5.
  • Nocturnal heartburn-free nights — 71% vs 42% (p<0.001) 4. Consistent with mechanism-of-action profile.
  • Patient-reported quality of life (RDQ) — numerical improvement did not reach the powered MCID at Week 8, though it did at Week 12 5. Frame as "trended favourably" in the briefing.

3EU site heterogeneity

Between-country I² was modest (28%). Two Italian sites showed a larger placebo response that narrowed the treatment delta; sensitivity analyses excluding them recovered the headline effect (Δ 30.9 pp) 6.

4What I'd flag for the EMA briefing

  • RDQ at Week 8 is the only secondary that missed its powered threshold — lead with the Week 12 result and pre-empt the CHMP question.
  • Italian site variance is defensible via the prespecified sensitivity analysis; include the forest plot (Fig. 14-3) in the main body, not the appendix.
  • Severe-subgroup benefit is the single strongest framing for the claim of moderate-to-severe indication.
